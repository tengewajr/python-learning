WEEK 5 – FINAL MOTOR BEHAVIOR ANALYSIS

1. What does normal motor behavior look like?

In this simulated dataset, normal motor behavior is characterized by
average Temperature of 65.66, Current of 8.56 and RPM of 1445.94.
Individual readings fluctuate considerably, while rolling averages provide
a smoother representation of the underlying behavior.

2. What abnormal behavior did you identify?

Using the selected learning thresholds, 49 out of 100 readings were
flagged as potential anomalies. The analysis also identified a maximum
temperature increase of 22.78, maximum RPM decrease of 80 RPM, and
maximum current increase of 2.73 A. These indicate unusual behavior but
do not confirm motor failure.

3. Which sensors appear to be related?

Current and RPM showed the strongest positive relationship (r = 0.12),
while Temperature and RPM showed the strongest negative relationship
(r = -0.08). Both relationships were very weak, indicating no strong
linear relationships between the measured sensors in this dataset.

4. Which sensors appear most useful for identifying abnormal behavior?

Current and RPM produced the highest numbers of threshold-based anomaly
flags, with 23 and 22 respectively. Temperature also provides useful
information, especially when analyzed using changes and rolling trends.
A stronger conclusion would require real motor data and model-based
feature evaluation.

5. What new features did you create?

The engineered features were Temperature Change, RPM Change,
5-reading Rolling Temperature, 5-reading Rolling Current, and a binary
Anomaly Flag.

6. If building an ML model to predict failure, which features would you use?

I would consider Temperature, Current, Voltage and RPM together with
Temperature Change, RPM Change, Rolling Temperature and Rolling Current.
Anomaly could also be considered if calculated without introducing
target leakage.

7. What additional information would make prediction more reliable?

More reliable prediction would require longer-term real motor data,
vibration and acoustic measurements, electrical measurements, motor
load or torque information, operating conditions, maintenance history,
actual fault records and accurately labelled failure events. Data from
different motor types and operating conditions would also improve
reliability.
