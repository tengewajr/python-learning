WEEK 5 – DAY 1
TIME-SERIES ANALYSIS

Dataset:
The motor sensor dataset contains 100 readings recorded at one-minute
intervals from 09:00 to 10:39 on 4 September 2026.

1. Which sensor changes the most over time?

The sensors showed different magnitudes of change over time. The largest
observed temperature increase was 22.78, the largest RPM decrease was
80 RPM, and the largest current increase was 2.73 A. Since these
variables use different units, their raw changes cannot be directly
compared to determine which sensor is most variable without normalization.

2. Which sensor appears to be the most stable?

RPM appears relatively stable at the hourly scale because its hourly
average changed only slightly from 1445.42 RPM during the 09:00–09:59
period to 1446.72 RPM during the available 10:00–10:39 period.

3. Are there periods where several sensors change at the same time?

Yes, multiple sensor variables can change during the same monitoring
period. The hourly averages show that temperature and current increased
between the two periods. A minute-by-minute comparison would be required
to identify exact timestamps where several sensors change simultaneously.

4. Why might the sequence of readings matter when monitoring a motor?

The sequence matters because motor condition develops over time. A single
sensor reading may not reveal an important problem, whereas a sequence
of readings can reveal trends, gradual deterioration, sudden changes and
relationships between sensors. These temporal patterns can be useful for
anomaly detection and predictive maintenance.
